About Target Corporation

Founded in 1902 as the Dayton Dry Goods Company; the first Target store opened in 1962. Michael Fiddelke, previously chief operating officer, became CEO in February 2026, succeeding Brian Cornell. Target occupies an unusual position in US retail — cheaper than department stores but with more design credibility than pure discounters, built on exclusive owned brands and designer collaborations. Roughly half of revenue comes from discretionary categories, which makes it more cyclical than a grocery-weighted competitor.

Investment case

  • Owned brands generate higher margins than national brands and cannot be price-compared directly.
  • Stores double as fulfilment centres, which makes same-day delivery economics far better than pure e-commerce.
  • Strong brand affinity with a higher-income customer than most discount retailers.

Key risks

  • Heavy discretionary mix means sales fall faster than grocery-led peers when consumers trade down.
  • Walmart has been taking share from higher-income shoppers, historically Target's core.
  • Inventory and shrink management problems have repeatedly caught the company out.
