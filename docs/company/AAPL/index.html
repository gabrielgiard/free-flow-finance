About Apple Inc.

Founded on 1 April 1976 by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak and Ronald Wayne. Tim Cook has been CEO since 2011, headquartered at Apple Park in Cupertino. The iPhone is roughly half of revenue; the rest comes from Mac, iPad, Apple Watch, AirPods and the Vision Pro headset. The Services business — the App Store, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV+, Apple Pay and the licensing payment Google makes to remain the default search engine in Safari — carries far higher margins than hardware and is the main driver of profit growth. Apple designs its own M-series and A-series silicon, manufactured by TSMC.

Investment case

  • Services revenue carries roughly 75% gross margins and grows without requiring a hardware refresh cycle.
  • An installed base above two billion active devices is the largest premium consumer platform ever assembled.
  • Buys back enormous quantities of stock every year, which compounds earnings per share even in flat revenue years.

Key risks

  • Apple Intelligence has lagged competitors, and the perception that Apple is behind on AI has been a persistent overhang.
  • The Google search default payment is a significant share of Services profit and is directly threatened by antitrust remedies.
  • China is both a large market and the manufacturing base, which makes tariff and geopolitical risk two-sided.

What the street says

The most widely owned stock in the world and correspondingly well covered. On 27 July 2026 Apple reclaimed the title of most valuable company from NVIDIA — notably on capital discipline rather than AI spending, at the same moment the market began punishing heavy capex elsewhere. The live debate is whether the AI gap matters commercially or whether the installed base makes Apple a fast follower with no penalty.
