About McDonald's Corporation

The first McDonald's franchise was opened by Ray Kroc in Des Plaines, Illinois in 1955, building on a restaurant run by the McDonald brothers in San Bernardino. Chris Kempczinski is CEO, headquartered in Chicago. Around 95% of its roughly 43,000 restaurants are franchised, so McDonald's primarily collects rent and royalties rather than running restaurants. It is one of the largest commercial property owners in the world, because it typically owns or controls the land under its franchised locations — a detail that explains much of its financial resilience.

Investment case

  • The franchise and property model produces rent-like cash flows with very high margins and low capital needs.
  • Value positioning means McDonald's tends to gain traffic when consumers trade down.
  • Digital ordering, delivery and loyalty have raised average check and visit frequency.

Key risks

  • Low-income consumer traffic weakened notably in 2025 and 2026 as household budgets tightened.
  • Around $52B of net debt, held deliberately, is more expensive to service as rates stay higher.
  • GLP-1 drugs reduce fast food consumption among an expanding patient population.
