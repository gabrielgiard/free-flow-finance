About PepsiCo, Inc.

Formed in 1965 by merging Pepsi-Cola with Frito-Lay, headquartered in Purchase, New York. Ramon Laguarta is CEO. Unlike Coca-Cola, PepsiCo is roughly 55% snacks and only 45% beverages, and the snack business — Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Ruffles, Tostitos and Quaker Oats — is significantly more profitable and faster growing than the drinks. It owns most of its own bottling, which lowers margins but gives it control of distribution. Gatorade dominates sports drinks and the portfolio has been shifted toward lower-sugar and functional products.

Investment case

  • Snacks are structurally more attractive than carbonated drinks, with better growth and less regulatory attention.
  • Combined snack and beverage distribution gives real leverage with retailers.
  • International markets provide a long runway where per-capita consumption is still low.

Key risks

  • North American snack volumes have declined as consumers push back on price increases.
  • GLP-1 adoption reduces snacking specifically, which hits Frito-Lay more than beverages.
  • Owning bottling means more capital intensity and lower margins than Coca-Cola's model.
