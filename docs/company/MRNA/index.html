About Moderna, Inc.

Founded in 2010 to develop messenger RNA therapeutics, an approach with no approved products until COVID-19 vaccines validated it at extraordinary speed. Stéphane Bancel has been CEO since 2011. Revenue collapsed from pandemic peaks and Moderna is now spending heavily to prove mRNA works beyond COVID — in RSV, influenza, and most importantly individualised cancer vaccines.

Investment case

  • The mRNA platform allows far faster development cycles than conventional biologics.
  • Individualised neoantigen therapy intismeran, with Merck's Keytruda, met its primary recurrence-free survival endpoint in the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 melanoma trial in August 2026.
  • Cash plus $2.6 billion of convertible notes raised in August 2026 funds oncology expansion and several more years of development.

Key risks

  • Deeply lossmaking, with revenue far below the cost base built during the pandemic.
  • COVID vaccine demand continues to decline and is now seasonal at best.
  • After the August 2026 melanoma readout the valuation assumes regulatory approval and commercial success for cancer vaccines that have not yet been filed.

What the street says

The August 2026 Phase 3 melanoma success has turned the debate from whether the cancer platform works to how large it can become, and the shares traded around $143 by late August. Moderna joins the Nasdaq-100 on 9 October 2026. Bears still point to a heavily lossmaking respiratory business and the dilution embedded in the new convertible.
