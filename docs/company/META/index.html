About Meta Platforms, Inc.

Founded by Mark Zuckerberg in a Harvard dorm room in 2004 and renamed Meta in 2021. Zuckerberg still controls the company through supervoting shares. It operates Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger and Threads, reaching over three billion daily users, and monetises almost entirely through advertising. Reality Labs builds Quest headsets and Ray-Ban smart glasses at a large annual loss. Meta develops the open-weight Llama model family and, in July 2026, unveiled Meta Compute — a plan to sell surplus AI computing capacity to outside developers, turning part of a roughly $115-135 billion annual infrastructure bill into a potential revenue line.

Investment case

  • Advertising monetisation keeps improving because AI ranking makes each impression more valuable — this is the clearest example of AI paying for itself.
  • Three billion daily users creates a distribution asset that cannot be replicated at any price.
  • Instagram Reels and WhatsApp business messaging are still early in monetisation.

Key risks

  • Reality Labs has burned tens of billions with no line of sight to profitability.
  • Unveiling Meta Compute in July 2026 to resell surplus capacity was read by some as an admission that infrastructure spend has outrun near-term need — the opposite of the message Zuckerberg wants the market to take from record capex.
  • Regulatory pressure on teen safety and data use is persistent across the US and EU.

What the street says

Generally well regarded on advertising execution, with the argument concentrated on capital allocation. The July 2026 quarter crystallised it: revenue grew 27% to $60.8bn and beat expectations, yet earnings per share rose only around 1% and the shares fell, because 2026 capital spending guidance was raised again to $125-145bn. Revenue growth without earnings growth is the gap the market is now focused on.
