About Coinbase Global, Inc.

Founded in 2012 by Brian Armstrong and Fred Ehrsam and listed by direct listing in 2021. The company is remote-first with no formal headquarters. Coinbase is the largest regulated cryptocurrency exchange in the United States, earning transaction fees from retail and institutional trading. It also runs Coinbase Prime for institutions, provides custody for most US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds, operates the Base layer-2 blockchain, and earns a large share of the interest income on USDC reserves through its partnership with Circle. It announced a partnership with JPMorgan in July 2026.

Investment case

  • Regulatory clarity in the US has improved substantially, and Coinbase is the most compliant large venue.
  • Subscription and services revenue, particularly stablecoin interest, is far less volatile than trading fees.
  • Custody for the majority of US spot bitcoin ETFs is a recurring, high-margin franchise.

Key risks

  • Transaction revenue is directly tied to crypto trading volumes, which collapse in bear markets.
  • Interest income on USDC reserves falls if the Fed cuts rates — though the 2026 hawkish shift is currently a tailwind.
  • Competition from traditional brokerages and offshore exchanges compresses fee rates.
