About RTX Corporation

Formed in 2020 by merging Raytheon with United Technologies, with roots to 1922. Christopher Calio is CEO. Three businesses: Collins Aerospace, which makes aircraft interiors, avionics and landing systems; Pratt & Whitney, which builds the GTF engines for the Airbus A320neo and the F135 for the F-35 fighter; and Raytheon, which produces Patriot and NASAMS air defence systems, Tomahawk and AMRAAM missiles. Air defence demand has risen sharply given conflicts in Europe and the Middle East, and the backlog is at record levels.

Investment case

  • Air and missile defence demand is at generational highs, and the backlog extends years out.
  • Commercial aerospace aftermarket is recovering strongly as flight hours rise.
  • The defence backlog provides revenue visibility that few industrials can match.

Key risks

  • The Pratt & Whitney GTF powder metal contamination issue has cost billions and grounded aircraft.
  • Government contracts often carry fixed-price terms, so cost inflation is absorbed by RTX not the customer.
  • Defence budgets are politically determined and can be cut with little warning.
