About The Procter & Gamble Company

Founded in 1837 in Cincinnati by William Procter, a candle maker, and James Gamble, a soap maker, who had married sisters. Jon Moeller is CEO. P&G owns a portfolio of consumer staples brands where each is a category leader: Tide and Ariel in laundry, Pampers in baby care, Gillette in grooming, Head & Shoulders and Pantene in hair care, Oral-B, Crest, Bounty, Charmin and SK-II. It deliberately exited dozens of smaller brands to concentrate on categories where scale and advertising spend create a durable advantage.

Investment case

  • Pricing power in staples is real — P&G raised prices through the inflation of 2021-23 with limited volume loss.
  • Advertising scale creates a barrier private labels cannot overcome in most of its categories.
  • Nearly seventy consecutive years of dividend increases, one of the longest records in the market.

Key risks

  • Volume growth is close to zero, so the story depends entirely on price and mix.
  • Private label share gains accelerate when consumers are stressed, and the 2026 oil shock is pressuring budgets.
  • GLP-1 weight loss drugs are reducing snacking and grooming category consumption in ways that are hard to model.
