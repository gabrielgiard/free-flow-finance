About Qualcomm Incorporated

Founded in 1985 by Irwin Jacobs and six colleagues in San Diego. Cristiano Amon is CEO. Two businesses: QCT, which sells Snapdragon system-on-chip processors and modems for Android phones, cars and PCs; and QTL, which licenses Qualcomm's foundational CDMA and 5G patents and collects a royalty on essentially every 3G/4G/5G handset sold worldwide, regardless of whose chip is inside. The licensing business is small in revenue but enormous in profit. Qualcomm is diversifying into automotive cockpit and ADAS chips and Snapdragon X PC processors.

Investment case

  • QTL licensing is close to pure profit and is contractually tied to global handset volumes rather than Qualcomm's own share.
  • Automotive design-win pipeline is large and books years of revenue in advance.
  • Trades at a persistent discount to the semiconductor group despite comparable returns on capital.

Key risks

  • Apple has been shipping its own modems and will eventually remove a large chunk of QCT revenue.
  • Handset volumes are mature; a projected collapse in global smartphone demand was one of the June 2026 selloff triggers.
  • Licensing revenue depends on renewing agreements with Chinese OEMs, which is a recurring negotiation risk.
