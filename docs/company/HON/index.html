About Honeywell International

Traces to a 1906 thermostat business and the 1999 merger of AlliedSignal and Honeywell. Vimal Kapur is chairman and CEO. After spinning off Solstice Advanced Materials and then Honeywell Aerospace, which began trading separately on Nasdaq in June 2026, the remaining company operates as Honeywell Technologies, a pure-play automation business. Building Automation supplies control and safety systems for hospitals, airports, data centres and commercial buildings. Process Automation and Technology serves LNG plants, refineries, life sciences and utilities, and added Johnson Matthey's Catalyst Technologies business in 2026. Industrial Automation is the third segment. It retains a large stake in Quantinuum, the quantum computing company that listed in June 2026.

Investment case

  • Management describes it as the only large pure-play automation company in public markets, so it can now be valued against automation peers rather than as a conglomerate.
  • Around a quarter of revenue sits in high-growth markets such as data centres, semiconductor fabs and life sciences that management says are growing roughly 10% a year.
  • The retained Quantinuum stake gives exposure to a leading quantum computing company that carries almost no weight in the valuation.

Key risks

  • Two spin-offs in under two years leave stranded costs and execution risk for the smaller remaining company.
  • Industrial automation demand is tied to capital spending cycles that have been soft.
  • Guidance for 3–4% organic sales growth in 2026 is modest for a company now marketed as a growth-focused automation pure-play.
