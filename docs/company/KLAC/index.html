About KLA Corporation

Formed by the 1997 merger of KLA Instruments (1975) and Tencor. Richard Wallace has been CEO since 2006. KLA dominates process control — the inspection and metrology tools that find defects on wafers during manufacturing. It holds well over half the market. As transistor structures get smaller and more three-dimensional, yield becomes the hardest problem in chipmaking, and every extra process step needs another inspection point. That makes KLA's content per wafer rise with complexity, and its gross margins are the highest in equipment at around 60%.

Investment case

  • Over 50% share of process control with no serious challenger, and the highest gross margins in semiconductor equipment.
  • Yield matters more as nodes shrink, so KLA's revenue per wafer rises structurally with each generation.
  • Service revenue grows with the installed base and is essentially non-cyclical.

Key risks

  • Same cyclical exposure as the rest of wafer fab equipment, with a higher multiple to lose.
  • China export restrictions removed a meaningful revenue stream.
  • Small float and high share price make the stock unusually volatile on order news.
