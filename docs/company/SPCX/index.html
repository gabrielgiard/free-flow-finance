About SpaceX (Space Exploration Technologies)

Founded in 2002 by Elon Musk with the stated goal of making humanity multiplanetary. Headquartered at Starbase, Texas, with Gwynne Shotwell as President and COO. SpaceX completed the largest IPO in history on 12 June 2026, pricing at $135 and closing its first day at $160.95 for a market capitalisation above $2 trillion. Three businesses: launch, using the reusable Falcon 9 and the Starship system; Starlink satellite broadband, which passed 10.3 million customers across 160 markets and generated about 61% of 2025 revenue; and artificial intelligence, following the acquisition of xAI in February 2026.

Investment case

  • Reusable rockets have cut launch costs by roughly an order of magnitude, and no competitor has matched it at scale.
  • Starlink subscribers more than doubled from 4.6 million at the end of 2024 to 10.3 million by March 2026.
  • Vertical integration from rocket to satellite to ground terminal means SpaceX controls its own cost curve.

Key risks

  • The xAI segment burned $7.72 billion in the first quarter of 2026 alone and posted a $2.47 billion operating loss.
  • The stock traded as high as $225.64 in its first weeks on the market and has since fallen sharply, so volatility is extreme and the price discovery process is not finished.
  • The valuation requires believing revenue can grow from $18.7 billion in 2025 to something enormous; Musk has publicly suggested roughly $1 trillion by 2030, which no credible model supports.

What the street says

Genuinely split, and unusually well-documented for a five-week-old listing. CFRA's Keith Snyder initiated at Sell with a $115 target, arguing the price already assumes Starship succeeds commercially before there's evidence it will. Morningstar's Nicolas Owens ran his own DCF and landed at $63, implying the market is pricing in outcomes his model can't get to. On the other side, Wedbush's Dan Ives initiated at Outperform with a $190 target and called the IPO itself a 'Goldilocks outcome' that clears the runway for OpenAI and Anthropic to follow. ARK Invest's published Monte Carlo model centres on a $2.5 trillion 2030 enterprise value, with bear and bull cases of $1.7 trillion and $3.1 trillion. Line up the price targets and the spread runs from roughly $830 billion to $2.5 trillion-plus — the widest disagreement on any name we cover, and a reasonable one given how little trading history exists.
