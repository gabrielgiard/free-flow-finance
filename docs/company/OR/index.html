About L'Oréal S.A.

Founded in 1909 by chemist Eugène Schueller, who invented a safe synthetic hair dye. Nicolas Hieronimus is CEO; the Bettencourt Meyers family and Nestlé remain large shareholders. L'Oréal is the largest beauty company in the world across four divisions: Luxe with Lancôme, Yves Saint Laurent Beauté and Armani; Consumer Products with Maybelline, Garnier and L'Oréal Paris; Dermatological Beauty with La Roche-Posay, CeraVe and Vichy, the fastest-growing division; and Professional Products for salons. Prices quoted in euros.

Investment case

  • Dermatological beauty, particularly CeraVe and La Roche-Posay, is growing fast and is recommended by dermatologists, which is powerful marketing.
  • Beauty has proven remarkably resilient in downturns — the so-called lipstick effect is real.
  • Research and development spending is far higher than peers, producing genuine formulation advantages.

Key risks

  • Chinese demand for premium beauty has been weak and China was the main growth engine.
  • Competition from social-media-native brands is intense in colour cosmetics.
  • The valuation has historically been high and leaves little room for growth disappointment.
