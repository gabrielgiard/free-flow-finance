About Linde plc

Founded in 1879 by Carl von Linde, who invented industrial refrigeration; merged with Praxair in 2018 to create the largest industrial gas company in the world. Sanjiv Lamba is CEO. Linde produces and distributes oxygen, nitrogen, argon, hydrogen and carbon dioxide to steelmakers, chemical plants, electronics fabs, hospitals and food processors. The business model is unusually attractive: gases are supplied under fifteen-year take-or-pay contracts from plants built on the customer's site, so revenue is contracted and switching is effectively impossible.

Investment case

  • On-site plants under long take-or-pay contracts produce utility-like revenue with industrial margins.
  • Semiconductor fabs consume enormous volumes of ultra-pure gases, so Linde benefits directly from the chip build-out.
  • Clean hydrogen projects offer long-dated growth optionality with contracted offtake.

Key risks

  • Industrial production volumes are cyclical and Europe has been in a manufacturing recession.
  • Energy costs are a large input, and the 2026 oil and gas spike compresses margins until contracts reprice.
  • The valuation is high for an industrial and rarely offers an attractive entry point.
