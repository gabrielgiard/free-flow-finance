About Fiserv, Inc.

Founded in 1984. Takis Georgakopoulos became chief executive in June 2026, promoted from co-president after Mike Lyons left abruptly for Truist eighteen months into the job. The shares fell over 8% on the announcement and are down more than 70% over the year, so this is a turnaround with the credibility of its own targets in question. Fiserv provides the core banking software that thousands of smaller US banks and credit unions run on, plus Clover, a point-of-sale system for small merchants that has become the growth engine. Core banking systems are famously difficult to replace — many run on decades-old architecture precisely because migration risk is so high.

Investment case

  • Core banking contracts run for many years and switching is a multi-year project banks rarely undertake.
  • Clover has taken meaningful share in small business point-of-sale against Square.
  • Payment volumes grow with nominal consumer spending regardless of unit growth.

Key risks

  • Substantial debt taken on through years of acquisitions.
  • Small business formation and health drive Clover, making it economically sensitive.
  • Fintech entrants keep attacking the most profitable parts of the payments chain.

What the street says

Sentiment weakened further after second-quarter 2026 results missed estimates and the full-year organic revenue outlook was cut to between -1% and flat, with adjusted EPS guided to $7.20–$7.40. Management blamed timing rather than lost business, but the credibility of the reset is still debated. Reports of preliminary talks to sell the STAR debit network to large US banks lifted the shares in August 2026, though no deal has been announced and analysts are sceptical one happens.
