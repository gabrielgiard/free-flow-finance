About Applied Materials

Founded in 1967, the largest semiconductor equipment company by revenue. Gary Dickerson has led it since 2013. Applied sells the deposition, etch, chemical mechanical planarization and ion implantation tools that build transistor structures layer by layer, plus inspection systems and display equipment. Its Applied Global Services arm provides parts, upgrades and maintenance across the installed base. Gate-all-around transistors and advanced packaging both increase the number of process steps per wafer, which mechanically increases Applied's content.

Investment case

  • Broadest tool portfolio in the industry — customers can source most of a fab line from one vendor.
  • Gate-all-around and backside power delivery raise the number of process steps, increasing Applied's dollar content per wafer.
  • Services revenue on a large installed base cushions the downside in a capex trough.

Key risks

  • China was historically a very large revenue share and export controls have structurally reduced it.
  • The stock fell 7.35% in a single session during the July 2026 selloff — this is a high-beta way to own the capex cycle.
  • Equipment orders lead revenue by several quarters, so the reported numbers look best right as the cycle turns.

What the street says

Fiscal Q3 2026 revenue of $9.12bn, up 25%, beat consensus, and the Q4 guide of $10.25bn at the midpoint was well above the $9.62bn expected, with DRAM and advanced packaging driving growth. The stock nonetheless de-rated, falling about 22% in the month to mid-September and another 6% on 14 September 2026, and Morgan Stanley cut its target while raising 2027 estimates. The debate is now how long the AI-led equipment upcycle lasts, alongside how much of the China revenue loss is permanent.
