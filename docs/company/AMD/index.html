About Advanced Micro Devices

Founded in 1969 by Jerry Sanders and seven colleagues from Fairchild Semiconductor. Lisa Su took over in 2014 with the stock near $3 and rebuilt it around TSMC manufacturing and a chiplet architecture. Products: EPYC server CPUs, Instinct MI-series AI accelerators, Ryzen desktop and laptop processors, Radeon graphics, Xilinx FPGAs and the semi-custom chips inside PlayStation and Xbox. In Q1 2026 AMD's data-centre revenue overtook Intel's for the first time.

Investment case

  • Passing Intel in data-centre revenue in Q1 2026 was a genuine structural milestone, not a quarterly blip.
  • Instinct MI-series gives hyperscalers a real second source, and they are willing to pay to avoid single-vendor dependence.
  • Server CPU share gains compound — once a datacentre validates EPYC, refresh cycles favour the incumbent.

Key risks

  • ROCm software remains far behind CUDA, and that gap is the whole reason NVIDIA keeps its pricing.
  • The stock rose 30% in September 2026 alone on AI-agent CPU demand and the World Labs deal; at these levels execution has to be close to flawless, and a cautious guide, as on 4 August 2026, still triggers double-digit drops.
  • AMD is fabless and competes for the same TSMC leading-edge capacity as NVIDIA and Apple.

What the street says

Q2 2026 revenue rose 50% to $11.5bn with data-centre revenue up 107% to $6.7bn, but a Q3 guide of about $13bn sat below some estimates and the shares fell more than 10% after hours. Sentiment then swung sharply, with the stock up 30% in September 2026 on expectations that AI agents such as Meta's Muse will lift server CPU demand, alongside the $8.2bn all-stock purchase of World Labs. Views remain split between those treating AMD as the clear number-two AI accelerator franchise and those arguing the valuation already prices in share gains that have not yet shown up in the numbers.
