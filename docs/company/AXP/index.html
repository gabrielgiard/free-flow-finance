About American Express Company

Founded in 1850 as a freight express business in Buffalo, New York, later moving into money orders and travellers cheques. Stephen Squeri is CEO. Amex is unusual because it is both the network and the lender — a closed-loop model that gives it direct data on cardholder spending. Revenue comes from merchant discount fees, annual card fees on premium products such as Platinum and Gold, and net interest on lending. Its customer base skews affluent, and the premium fee model has proven strikingly successful with younger high-earning consumers.

Investment case

  • Annual card fee revenue is recurring, growing and remarkably resilient — customers renew even in downturns.
  • The affluent customer base has better credit performance than the industry through every cycle.
  • The closed loop gives Amex spending data that open networks simply do not have.

Key risks

  • Merchant acceptance is still narrower than Visa and Mastercard, particularly outside the US.
  • Amex takes credit risk directly, unlike the networks, so a recession hits both volumes and provisions.
  • Travel and entertainment spending, its highest-margin category, is sensitive to geopolitical disruption.
