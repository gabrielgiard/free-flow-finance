About GlobalFoundries Inc.

Created in 2009 when AMD spun out its manufacturing operations, with Abu Dhabi's Mubadala as majority owner. Tim Breen became CEO in 2025. GlobalFoundries abandoned the race for leading-edge nodes in 2018 and instead specialises in mature and specialty processes — the chips that go into cars, phones' radio front ends, and industrial equipment, where reliability matters more than transistor density. Its US and European fabs give customers geographic diversification away from Taiwan.

Investment case

  • Geographic diversification is genuinely valuable to customers worried about Taiwan concentration.
  • Specialty processes have longer product lifecycles and less pricing pressure than leading edge.
  • Substantial government support, including a $375 million Commerce Department quantum R&D award finalised in September 2026 and an expected $300 million award for silicon photonics.

Key risks

  • Deliberately exited leading-edge manufacturing, so its AI exposure comes through adjacent areas such as optical networking, silicon photonics and packaging rather than the accelerators themselves.
  • Far smaller scale than TSMC, with correspondingly weaker margins.
  • Automotive and smartphone end markets have both been soft.

What the street says

Seen as a strategically useful but structurally lower-margin foundry, though AI-related optical networking demand is starting to change the growth story. June 2026 quarter revenue rose 6% year on year to $1.786 billion, above the top of guidance, and the October 2026 agreement to make silicon interposers for TSMC's CoWoS packaging in Malta, New York gives it a direct role in the US AI packaging supply chain.
