About Salesforce, Inc.

Founded in 1999 by Marc Benioff and three colleagues in a San Francisco apartment, with the explicit goal of ending packaged enterprise software. Benioff is still CEO. Salesforce is the largest customer relationship management platform, extended by acquisition into Slack, Tableau analytics, MuleSoft integration and Commerce Cloud. Its Agentforce product sells autonomous AI agents that handle customer service and sales tasks, priced by conversation rather than by seat — a deliberate move away from the per-seat model that AI arguably threatens.

Investment case

  • Very high gross retention — CRM is the system of record for the sales organisation and rarely gets ripped out.
  • Agentforce moves pricing to consumption, which protects against the risk that AI reduces the number of seats needed.
  • Operating margins have expanded substantially since activist investors arrived in 2023.

Key risks

  • Seat-based software is the category most directly threatened if AI agents reduce headcount in sales and support.
  • Growth has decelerated into single digits, which makes the multiple hard to defend on growth alone.
  • A long acquisition history means a large share of profit is adjusted for stock compensation and amortisation.

What the street says

Increasingly viewed as the most leveraged large-cap bet on the AI capital cycle continuing. Cloud infrastructure revenue grew 93% year over year in the June 2026 quarter and remaining performance obligations reached roughly $638bn, but a meaningful share is tied to prepaid or customer-supplied GPU arrangements. That makes Oracle the name most exposed if hyperscaler spending plateaus, which is precisely the fear that drove the July 2026 selloff.
