About Amazon.com, Inc.

Founded by Jeff Bezos in 1994 as an online bookstore run out of a Bellevue garage. Andy Jassy, who built AWS, became CEO in 2021. Three engines: North American and International retail, including third-party marketplace and Prime; Amazon Web Services, the largest public cloud, which generates the majority of operating profit; and advertising, now a very large business built on retail search placement. Amazon also designs its own Trainium and Inferentia AI chips and operates Kuiper satellite broadband.

Investment case

  • AWS is the profit engine and its backlog keeps extending as enterprises commit to multi-year AI capacity.
  • Advertising is a very high margin business bolted onto retail infrastructure that already exists.
  • Retail margins have been improving for several years as the regional fulfilment redesign matures.

Key risks

  • Capex is enormous and rising, which suppresses free cash flow in exactly the years the market wants to see it.
  • AWS growth has trailed Azure, raising questions about relative positioning in AI workloads.
  • Retail is exposed to consumer weakness, and higher oil prices in 2026 are pressuring both consumers and logistics costs.

What the street says

Broadly favoured as a way to own cloud plus advertising plus retail optionality. The July 2026 quarter answered the central question emphatically: revenue passed $200bn for the first time and AWS grew 37%, its fastest in eighteen quarters. Amazon also raised its 2026 capital plan to roughly $220bn, so the free cash flow drag from the AI build-out remains the main pushback.
