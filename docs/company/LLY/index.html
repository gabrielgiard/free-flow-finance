About Eli Lilly and Company

Founded in 1876 by Colonel Eli Lilly, a Union Army veteran and pharmaceutical chemist, in Indianapolis, where it is still headquartered. David Ricks has been CEO since 2017. Lilly was the first company to mass-produce insulin and later the polio vaccine. Today it is defined by the incretin franchise: Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity, both tirzepatide. Its oral GLP-1 pill, Foundayo, received FDA approval on 1 April 2026, opening the market to patients unwilling to inject. Other products include Verzenio in oncology and donanemab for Alzheimer's.

Investment case

  • The obesity market is one of the largest addressable opportunities in pharmaceutical history and Lilly has the best-in-class molecule.
  • Oral GLP-1 approval in April 2026 removes the injection barrier and expands the treatable population enormously.
  • Manufacturing capacity investment has been aggressive and early, which has been the binding constraint for the whole category.

Key risks

  • Payer pushback on pricing is intensifying as obesity drug spending becomes a visible line item in health budgets.
  • Compounded and counterfeit GLP-1 supply undercuts pricing in the cash-pay segment.
  • Patent expiry on tirzepatide arrives in the 2030s and the next franchise has to be built before then.
