About Pfizer Inc.

Founded in 1849 by cousins Charles Pfizer and Charles Erhart in Brooklyn. Albert Bourla has been CEO since 2019 and oversaw the Comirnaty COVID-19 vaccine, which briefly made Pfizer the highest-revenue pharmaceutical company in the world before demand collapsed. The company spent much of that windfall on acquisitions, most significantly $43bn for Seagen in 2023 to build an antibody-drug conjugate oncology franchise. The central question is whether that pipeline can offset a heavy patent cliff.

Investment case

  • Seagen gives Pfizer a genuine position in antibody-drug conjugates, one of the most productive areas in oncology.
  • The dividend yield is among the highest in large-cap pharma and is covered by current cash flow.
  • Expectations are low enough that pipeline surprises are asymmetric to the upside.

Key risks

  • A severe patent cliff through the late 2020s affecting Eliquis, Prevnar and several others.
  • The COVID revenue collapse was faster and deeper than management guided, which damaged credibility.
  • Heavy debt load taken on to fund Seagen limits further business development.
