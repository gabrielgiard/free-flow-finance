About Berkshire Hathaway Inc.

Originally a New England textile mill dating to 1839; Warren Buffett took control in 1965 and converted it into a holding company. Greg Abel formally succeeded Buffett as CEO on 1 January 2026, the first planned leadership change in Berkshire's history, with Buffett staying on as chairman. Berkshire owns operating businesses outright — GEICO insurance, BNSF Railway, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, Precision Castparts, See's Candies, Dairy Queen and dozens more — and holds a large listed equity portfolio, historically concentrated in Apple, American Express, Coca-Cola and Bank of America. The insurance float, premiums collected before claims are paid, provides low-cost capital that funds acquisitions. Its cash pile reached a record of roughly $397B by the end of the first quarter of 2026.

Investment case

  • An enormous cash position is a genuine asset in a market where credit is tightening — it buys optionality nobody else has.
  • Insurance float functions as permanent, low-cost leverage, which is structurally advantaged financing.
  • Extreme diversification across the real economy makes earnings unusually resilient.

Key risks

  • The succession from Buffett to Abel is the largest untested variable in the company's history.
  • Size is now the enemy of returns — moving the needle requires acquisitions few targets can absorb.
  • A large listed equity portfolio means reported earnings swing violently with mark-to-market rules.
