About Intuit Inc.

Founded in 1983 by Scott Cook and Tom Proulx to make personal finance software. Sasan Goodarzi has been CEO since 2019. Intuit owns TurboTax and QuickBooks, giving it dominant positions in US consumer tax filing and small business accounting, plus Credit Karma and Mailchimp. The businesses are unusually defensible: switching accounting software mid-year is painful, and tax filing is an annual habit reinforced by having last year's return already stored.

Investment case

  • QuickBooks is the default small business accounting standard in the US, with very low churn.
  • TurboTax benefits from an annual recurring habit and stored prior-year data.
  • AI-assisted tax and bookkeeping is a natural fit that could raise both pricing and attach rates.

Key risks

  • Persistent political pressure in the US for free government-run tax filing.
  • Small business formation and health drive QuickBooks, so a recession hits directly.
  • Fiscal 2027 guidance of 9–10% revenue growth, with TurboTax at 2–3% and Mailchimp flat to down, marks a clear slowdown from 14% in fiscal 2026.

What the street says

Still regarded as a high-quality software franchise, but expectations were reset in August 2026 when fiscal 2027 revenue guidance came in below consensus and the shares fell about 12% in early trading. Management cited weaker Mailchimp growth, a shrinking desktop ecosystem and deliberate changes to TurboTax, and Stifel warned the changes will take longer to work through.
