About Visa Inc.

Began in 1958 as the BankAmericard programme, became an independent association in 1970 and listed in 2008 in what was then the largest US IPO. Ryan McInerney is CEO. Visa does not lend or issue cards — it operates VisaNet, the network that authorises, clears and settles transactions between banks and merchants, taking a small fee on each. Roughly 4.5 billion cards run on it across more than 200 countries. Growth areas include Visa Direct for real-time payouts, tokenised credentials, and value-added services in fraud and data analytics.

Investment case

  • Operating margins above 50% — this is one of the most profitable business models in existence.
  • A genuine two-sided network: merchants must accept Visa because consumers hold it, and vice versa.
  • It takes no credit risk, so a recession hits volumes but not the balance sheet.

Key risks

  • Interchange fee regulation is a live threat in the US, EU and several emerging markets.
  • Account-to-account payment rails, including instant bank transfer schemes, bypass the card network entirely.
  • Cross-border travel volumes are the highest-margin revenue and the most exposed to geopolitical disruption.
