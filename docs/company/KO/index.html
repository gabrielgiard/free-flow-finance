About The Coca-Cola Company

The Coca-Cola formula was created by pharmacist John Pemberton in 1886; the company was incorporated in 1892 in Atlanta, where it remains. Henrique Braun is CEO, having succeeded James Quincey. Coca-Cola mostly sells concentrate to independent bottlers rather than finished drinks, which is why its margins are far higher than a beverage manufacturer's. Its portfolio extends well beyond the flagship cola to Sprite, Fanta, Minute Maid, Dasani and smartwater, Powerade, Costa Coffee, Topo Chico and BodyArmor. It has been shifting deliberately toward zero-sugar and non-carbonated categories.

Investment case

  • The concentrate model means Coca-Cola captures brand economics while bottlers carry the capital intensity.
  • Unrivalled global distribution — the product is available in more places than almost any other manufactured good.
  • Pricing power in emerging markets provides an inflation hedge.

Key risks

  • Sugar taxes and health regulation keep expanding across markets.
  • GLP-1 drugs suppress appetite and calorie consumption, which is a slow structural headwind for the category.
  • A strong dollar reduces the value of the large share of profit earned overseas.
