About ConocoPhillips

The modern company dates to the 2002 merger of Conoco and Phillips Petroleum, with roots back to 1917. Ryan Lance is CEO. In 2012 it spun off its refining business as Phillips 66, making ConocoPhillips the largest pure exploration and production company in the world — it finds and produces oil and gas but does not refine it. Core assets are the Permian, Eagle Ford and Bakken shale basins, Alaska including the Willow project, and liquefied natural gas positions in Qatar and at Port Arthur. It acquired Marathon Oil in 2024.

Investment case

  • Pure upstream exposure gives more direct leverage to oil prices than the integrated majors.
  • A deep inventory of low-cost shale drilling locations extends well beyond a decade.
  • LNG export positions diversify away from pure crude price exposure.

Key risks

  • No refining means no downstream cushion when crude prices fall — the earnings swing is much wider.
  • Shale decline rates are steep and require continuous reinvestment simply to hold production flat.
  • Alaska projects face persistent environmental and permitting litigation.
