About The Boeing Company

Founded in 1916 by William Boeing in Seattle. Kelly Ortberg became CEO in 2024, brought in to fix a company damaged by the 737 MAX crashes, a door plug failure in 2024, quality escapes and a costly strike. Boeing builds commercial aircraft — the 737 MAX, 787 Dreamliner and the delayed 777X — plus defence systems including the KC-46 tanker, F-15EX and satellites, and Global Services for maintenance and parts. It operates in a duopoly with Airbus, and the order backlog exceeds 5,500 aircraft.

Investment case

  • A backlog of over 5,500 aircraft represents years of contracted demand that cannot go elsewhere — Airbus has no spare capacity.
  • Free cash flow inflects sharply as production rates recover toward pre-crisis levels.
  • Global Services is a stable, high-margin business insulated from production problems.

Key risks

  • Certification remains the bottleneck: the FAA halted MAX 10 certification in September 2026 over a cockpit software flaw, and the 777-9 timeline is slipping into 2027.
  • Fixed-price defence contracts have generated repeated multi-billion-dollar charges.
  • Around $45B of net debt built up through years of cash burn constrains flexibility.

What the street says

A recovery story where the timing has repeatedly disappointed. The shares fell almost 7% on 28 September 2026 when the FAA halted MAX 10 certification over a software flaw, though the F/A-XX fighter award and a ratified engineers' contract removed two sources of uncertainty. Bulls point to the backlog and duopoly; bears note that Boeing has missed nearly every target it has set since 2019.
