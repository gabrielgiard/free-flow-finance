About GE Aerospace

General Electric was founded in 1892 through the merger of Thomas Edison's electric company with Thomson-Houston. After breaking into three companies, GE Aerospace is the surviving core, led by Larry Culp. It designs and manufactures jet engines: the LEAP engine, produced through the CFM joint venture with Safran, powers most narrowbody aircraft delivered today, and the GE9X powers the Boeing 777X. The economics of the business are in aftermarket services — engines are sold near cost and profit is earned over decades on spare parts and maintenance contracts.

Investment case

  • The installed base of roughly 45,000 commercial engines generates decades of high-margin aftermarket revenue.
  • Airlines are flying older aircraft longer because of delivery delays, which increases shop visits and spare parts demand.
  • LEAP holds a dominant position on the narrowbody platforms that will be built through the 2030s.

Key risks

  • Supply chain constraints have limited engine deliveries and the recovery has been slow.
  • Aviation demand is cyclical and directly exposed to fuel costs, which surged in 2026.
  • The stock has already re-rated substantially since the break-up and expectations are high.
