About Intel Corporation

Founded in 1968 by Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore, the company that made the microprocessor a commodity and then lost the leading edge to TSMC. Lip-Bu Tan became CEO in March 2025 and is running a turnaround with US government and strategic investor backing. Products: Core and Xeon processors, Gaudi AI accelerators, Altera FPGAs, and Intel Foundry, which is trying to sell manufacturing capacity to external customers on the 18A and 14A nodes. Up roughly 278% in the first half of 2026, then down 21% in seven trading days in July.

Investment case

  • Optionality is enormous — if 18A yields hold, Intel becomes the only leading-edge foundry outside Taiwan, and that is a strategic asset governments will pay for.
  • Government and strategic investment materially de-risked a balance sheet that looked genuinely stressed in 2024.
  • Even modest recovery in margins produces very large earnings swings from today's depressed base.

Key risks

  • Foundry is the whole bull case and it is still lossmaking at the operating line; leading-edge yields have to hold at volume before the economics work, and that is not yet proven.
  • Server CPU share fell from 72.8% to 66.8% year-over-year and AMD has now passed Intel in data-centre revenue.
  • Foundry needs external customers who are, so far, mostly running evaluation programmes rather than volume orders.

What the street says

The most contested large-cap semiconductor name, and one of the hardest hit in the July 2026 semiconductor selloff that erased over a trillion dollars of sector value. Bulls own it as a policy-backed option on Western manufacturing; bears note that the stock still prices in an execution record Intel has not delivered in a decade.
