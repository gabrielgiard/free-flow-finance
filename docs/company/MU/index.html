About Micron Technology

Founded in 1978 in the basement of a Boise dental office by Ward Parkinson and three partners. Sanjay Mehrotra has run it since 2017. Micron makes memory: DRAM, NAND flash, and critically HBM (high-bandwidth memory), the stacked DRAM that sits beside every AI accelerator. It is one of only three companies in the world that can make leading-edge DRAM at scale, alongside Samsung and SK Hynix. Quarterly revenue reached $41.5B in 2026 and the market cap crossed $1T in May 2026.

Investment case

  • HBM is sold out on multi-year contracts, which converts a historically brutal spot-priced commodity into something closer to a contracted business.
  • Only three credible suppliers worldwide, and adding DRAM capacity takes years and tens of billions of dollars.
  • US-based manufacturing makes Micron the structural winner from Western supply-chain policy.

Key risks

  • Memory is the most cyclical business in semiconductors. Every previous supercycle has ended in a savage price collapse.
  • Record profits alongside record multiple expansion is exactly what past cycle tops looked like, and the shares rose only about 1% after hours on a clear beat-and-raise on 30 September 2026.
  • Our model deliberately builds in a down year in FY3 because assuming a straight line here would not be credible.

What the street says

Still polarised, but the numbers keep moving the bulls' way: fiscal Q4 2026 revenue of $54.23bn beat consensus of $51.5bn, and Q1 FY27 guidance of $61.5bn, with non-GAAP gross margin near 86%, was well ahead of the $57.9bn expected. The muted share reaction shows the argument is now about duration rather than demand. Bulls say HBM contracting and strategic customer agreements have changed the cycle; bears point out that every memory cycle in history has been called structurally different at the top.
