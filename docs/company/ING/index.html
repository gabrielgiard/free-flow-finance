About ING Groep N.V.

Formed in 1991 from the merger of the Dutch insurer Nationale-Nederlanden and NMB Postbank, and since its 2009-2014 restructuring a pure bank. Steven van Rijswijk has been CEO since 2020. ING is the largest bank in the Netherlands and a leading retail and wholesale lender in Belgium and Germany, run largely as a digital bank with few branches. It is known for returning almost all spare capital to shareholders through dividends and large buybacks.

Investment case

  • A low-cost digital model gives one of the best cost-to-income ratios among Europe's big banks.
  • Excess capital is handed back steadily: payouts above 100% of profit in some years.
  • Fee income from mortgages, investment products and insurance distribution is growing faster than lending.

Key risks

  • Net interest income falls as the European Central Bank's rate cuts work through deposits.
  • Dutch mortgage and deposit markets are mature, so growth depends on Germany and wholesale.
  • As a big European bank it trades on the eurozone economy and on regulation of capital.

What the street says

Seen as one of Europe's steadier bank stories: modest growth, high returns on equity for a European lender and generous distributions. The debate is how far lower rates eat into income before fee growth catches up.
