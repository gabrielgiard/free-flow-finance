About ASML Holding N.V.

Spun out of Philips and ASM International in 1984 in Veldhoven, Netherlands, and led by Christophe Fouquet since 2024. ASML is the sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines — the roughly $200M systems required to print transistors below 7nm. Every advanced chip on earth is made on ASML equipment. Its newer High-NA EUV systems cost around $380M each. It also sells older DUV lithography, metrology and inspection tools, and earns a large recurring service revenue stream on an installed base that lasts decades.

Investment case

  • A genuine monopoly. There is no second supplier of EUV, and building one would take a decade and tens of billions.
  • Roughly a quarter of revenue is service and upgrades on installed machines — recurring, high margin and insensitive to the order cycle.
  • Every capex increase from TSMC, Samsung, Intel or Micron eventually lands as an ASML order.

Key risks

  • Order intake is lumpy to an extreme degree; a single quarter can miss badly on timing and say nothing about the trend.
  • Dutch and US export controls have closed most of the China market for advanced tools, and China was a large share of DUV sales.
  • Customer concentration is total — four companies effectively decide ASML's revenue.

What the street says

Broadly viewed as the highest-quality asset in the semiconductor supply chain, and High-NA is moving from pilot tool to production roadmap, with TSMC planning to introduce it into high-volume manufacturing from 2030. The shares fell 5% on 14 September 2026 in the AI 'pacing' selloff but have since recovered, with buybacks continuing (€442.5m in the week to 2 October 2026). Debate still focuses on order timing and how much China revenue is permanently gone versus deferred.
