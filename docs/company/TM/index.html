About Toyota Motor Corporation

Founded in 1937 by Kiichiro Toyoda as a spin-off from his father's loom manufacturing business. Kenta Kon, previously the finance chief, became president and CEO in April 2026, succeeding Koji Sato; headquartered in Toyota City, Japan. Toyota is the largest carmaker in the world by volume, selling around 10 million vehicles a year under the Toyota and Lexus brands, plus Daihatsu and Hino. It pioneered hybrid technology with the Prius in 1997, and that decision looks increasingly astute as hybrid demand has outpaced pure electric in several markets. The Toyota Production System is still the manufacturing benchmark studied worldwide.

Investment case

  • Hybrid leadership is paying off precisely as consumers hesitate on full battery electric vehicles.
  • Manufacturing efficiency produces industry-leading margins on mass-market vehicles.
  • Trades at a low multiple of earnings with a large balance sheet, and announced a record ¥1 trillion buyback, about 4.2% of shares, in August 2026.

Key risks

  • Late to battery electric vehicles relative to Chinese competitors, particularly in China itself.
  • Yen strength reduces the translated value of overseas profits.
  • US tariffs on imported vehicles are a direct cost, with Toyota projecting a ¥1.45 trillion drag for the current fiscal year.
