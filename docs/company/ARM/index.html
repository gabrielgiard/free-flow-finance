About Arm Holdings plc

Founded in Cambridge in 1990 as a joint venture between Acorn Computers, Apple and VLSI Technology. SoftBank bought it in 2016 and relisted it on Nasdaq in 2023; it remains majority SoftBank-owned. Rene Haas is CEO. Arm does not make chips — it licenses instruction set architectures and processor designs, then collects a royalty on every chip shipped using them. Roughly 99% of smartphones use Arm cores, and Arm-based server CPUs (AWS Graviton, NVIDIA Grace, Microsoft Cobalt) are taking real data-centre share.

Investment case

  • A royalty model with almost no cost of goods — incremental revenue drops through at extraordinary margins.
  • Arm v9 carries roughly double the royalty rate of v8, so the mix shift raises revenue without a single extra chip shipping.
  • Data-centre Arm adoption is the genuine new growth vector; every hyperscaler now ships an Arm server CPU.

Key risks

  • The valuation is extreme on any conventional measure, even after falling 12% in the week to 21 July 2026.
  • SoftBank's majority stake creates a persistent overhang and limits free float.
  • RISC-V is a free, open alternative and is gaining traction in embedded markets where royalty rates matter most.
