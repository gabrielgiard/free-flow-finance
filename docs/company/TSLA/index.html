About Tesla, Inc.

Founded in 2003 by Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning; Elon Musk joined as chairman in 2004 and has been CEO since 2008. Headquartered in Austin, Texas. Tesla makes the Model 3, Y, S and X, the Cybertruck and the Semi, at factories in Fremont, Shanghai, Berlin and Austin. Beyond vehicles it sells Powerwall and Megapack energy storage, operates the largest fast-charging network in the world, and is developing Full Self-Driving software, a robotaxi service and the Optimus humanoid robot. Energy storage has been the fastest-growing part of the business.

Investment case

  • Energy storage is growing far faster than vehicles and carries better margins — Megapack demand from data centres and grids is enormous.
  • The Supercharger network has become an industry standard that competitors now pay to access.
  • Full Self-Driving and robotaxi represent genuine optionality that no conventional automaker has.

Key risks

  • Automotive gross margins have compressed substantially as electric vehicle competition intensified, particularly from BYD.
  • The valuation cannot be justified on the car business alone; it requires believing in autonomy and robotics.
  • Key-person risk around Musk is real, and his attention is split across several companies including the newly public SpaceX.

What the street says

The most debated valuation in large-cap equities. Bulls underwrite autonomy and Optimus; bears value it as a carmaker and arrive at a fraction of the price. Our model gives partial credit to energy storage but does not attempt to value robotaxi, which is why the output sits well below market.
